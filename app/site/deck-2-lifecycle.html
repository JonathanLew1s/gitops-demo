Part 2 · Life of a change

The same change, then and now

Example: open a firewall port for a new service.

Then: spreadsheet & CABNow: git & Argo CD
DefineSomeone fills in a spreadsheet of rulesSomeone writes the rules as a small text file, on a branch
AskEmailed to a colleague, who adds it to the CAB agendaA pull request: the right reviewers are asked automatically
ReviewCAB reads a summary and trusts it matches the spreadsheetReviewers see the exact lines that will change. Checks run by themselves.
ApproveRecorded in the CAB minutesRecorded on the request. The author cannot approve their own.
ScheduleWait for the change windowMerge when approved, or on a schedule you choose
ImplementAn engineer retypes the rules by hand. Typos happen.Argo CD applies exactly what was approved. Nobody retypes anything.
TestA second person checks afterwardsAutomated tests first; a canary takes a share of traffic before everyone
Back outRollback plan, or an emergency changeRevert the commit. Emergency is the same path, just faster, still recorded.

Same process. Different tool: automated, controlled, and what was approved is exactly what is applied.

SAY: nothing about governance goes away. The CAB, the approval, the test and the rollback plan are all still there. What goes away is the retyping, the waiting and the "did it match what we approved?" doubt. ASK THE ROOM: how often has a change gone wrong because of a typo or a missed step, and not a bad design? KEY POINT: the thing that was reviewed is byte-for-byte the thing that was applied.
